  DOMAIN DIAGNOSTIC  —  one domain, or several genes in one record?
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The first version of this test counted DISTINCT Arabidopsis genes
  among the HSPs and called more than one a merge. That is wrong: for
  Ces14428 all fourteen matches landed on residues 1907-2232, the same
  stretch of protein, because fourteen members of one superfamily all
  match one prenyltransferase domain. Fourteen genes hitting ONE region
  is a domain. Different genes hitting DIFFERENT regions is a merge.
  So HSPs are merged into query regions first, and what matters is how
  much of the protein matches nothing at all.

    Ces14428 (2232 aa): 1 matched region(s), 326 aa matched, 1906 aa (85%) matching nothing
       1907-2232  ( 326 aa)  14 Arabidopsis genes, best: GGPS1, AT2G18620, GGPS3, AT3G29430
    -> ONE matched region. The 14 Arabidopsis genes matching it are one
       superfamily matching one domain, not several genes merged.
       What needs explaining is the 1906 aa (85%) that matches nothing:
       either a lineage-specific extension or an annotation that
       ran one gene into adjacent sequence. Either way this model
       is not one clean copy of anything, and the copy number is
       withheld rather than guessed.
